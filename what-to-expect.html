What to Expect

No surprises.
Just the work.

From the moment you reach out to the day your elevator is commissioned — here's exactly how this goes.

and reach out.

Good to know
If you submitted outside business hours, we'll have someone back to you by [TODD: e.g. "noon the next business day"]. We don't let submissions sit.
02
The first call

A conversation, not
a sales pitch.

We'll schedule a brief call — typically [TODD: e.g. "15 to 30 minutes"]. The goal isn't to sell you. It's to understand your project well enough to show up to your site fully prepared. We'll ask about your building, your timeline, and who else is involved in the decision.

What type of building or residence is it?
How many floors does the elevator need to serve?
Is this a new installation or an existing system?
What's your target timeline for completion?
Who else is involved in the decision — architect, GC, property manager?
Is there an existing elevator permit on file? (For service and modernization projects)
03
Site assessment

We come to you
before we quote you.

One of our QEI-certified technicians visits your location. We don't quote off a phone call. The site visit is where we gather what we need to give you an accurate proposal and the right system recommendation.

For commercial projects, we assess shaft dimensions, electrical capacity, structural load points, machine room access, and code compliance requirements for your jurisdiction. For residential projects, we evaluate your floor plan, structural framing, hoistway options, and the aesthetic direction you have in mind.

The visit typically takes [TODD: e.g. "one to two hours"]. We'll need access to the full building or relevant floors, and a point of contact who can answer questions on site.

What to have ready
Architectural drawings or floor plans if available. For existing systems: any prior inspection reports, permit history, or service records. For commercial: the building manager or property contact should be reachable during our visit.
04
Proposal & specifications

A full written scope.
No vague estimates.

Within [TODD: e.g. "five business days"] of the site visit, you receive a complete written proposal — system type, full scope of work, materials, timeline, and pricing. We specify exactly what we're doing and why. If you're comparing bids, our proposals are written so you can compare line by line.

If permits are required — and for most installations, they are — we handle the application. You don't manage that process. We'll let you know when permits are pulled and what they cover.

05
Permitting & scheduling

The quiet phase.
We handle it.

Once you sign, we pull the required permits and order equipment. Depending on your market, system type, and current lead times, this phase typically takes [TODD: e.g. "six to twelve weeks"]. We'll keep you updated at each milestone — permit approval, equipment order confirmation, delivery ETA, and scheduled start date.

During this time, you don't need to do much. We'll flag it if we need something from you. Most clients use this period to coordinate with their building manager or general contractor on site access and logistics.

A note on lead times
Equipment lead times vary. Some systems ship in [TODD: timeframe], others take longer depending on customization and manufacturer availability. We'll give you a specific ETA at the time of order and update you if anything changes.
06
Installation

Real construction.
Straight talk about what that means.

We want to be direct with you: elevator installation is real construction work. Our crew is skilled, certified, and professional — but they're also on a job site. They're not in khakis. The work area will look like a work area. There will be noise. There may be dust. The elevator will be out of service for the duration of the installation.

For commercial projects, we coordinate with your building manager to establish a work schedule that minimizes disruption to other tenants and building operations. We demarcate the work zone, coordinate freight elevator access where available, and communicate the daily schedule so your team isn't surprised.

For residential projects, we protect surrounding floors and surfaces as much as the work allows. We'll walk you through what the installation days will look like before we start.

A typical residential installation takes [TODD: e.g. "three to five days"] once work begins. Commercial projects vary significantly by system type and building complexity — your project manager will give you a specific timeline in the proposal. 07
Inspection & commissioning

The elevator doesn't move
until it passes.

Before we hand anything over, the elevator goes through a state inspection by a licensed elevator inspector — independent from our team. Our QEI-certified technicians are on site for the inspection and address any items on the spot. This is standard, expected, and part of the process we manage for you.

Once it passes, we commission the system — a full operational test sequence run with you or your representative present. We want you to see it working before we call the job complete.

What you receive at completion
State inspection certificate, warranty documentation, full maintenance record, and a copy of the as-built specifications. For commercial clients, we provide whatever documentation your building management or compliance team requires.
08
After the job

The same team.
For the life of the elevator.

Handoff isn't goodbye. We walk you through the system, answer questions, and make sure you know how to reach us. Most clients move into a maintenance contract — scheduled service, annual inspections, and priority response if something comes up.

When you call us six months after installation, you're not starting over with a stranger. You're calling the same team that built it. That's not marketing language. It's how we've kept clients like Brown-Forman and the Speed Art Museum coming back for years.

Emergency service is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week across all four markets. You'll have a direct number, not a queue.
